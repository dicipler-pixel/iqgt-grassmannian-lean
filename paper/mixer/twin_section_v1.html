Two mirrors, two tips

Two tip-and-tail systems face each other. Each surface is a tail: the plane of its two lower levels, held by its outer gap. Each tip is the single lowest direction, held only by the small inner gap. A's tip lands on B's surface and pushes B's levels; B's tip lands on A's and pushes back.

Only A is pushed. With the coupling at zero, B's tip stays still. Raise it and B's tip starts answering A's through the light line, and A's tip feels B's answer. The two surfaces barely move either way: the outer gaps hold the tails while the tips trade motion. Narrow the outer gap and the surfaces start to tilt as well.
