3 Spectral Realization of Tangent Deformations

For clarity, we first derive the explicit spectral formulas in the case of an isolated discrete spectrum; the operator statements extend to the general setting without change (Remark 3.8). To evaluate the intrinsic geometric tensor and its components, the abstract tangent vectors must be resolved into explicit, computable spectral identities. This section evaluates the contour integrals that define the first-order response of a spectral projector under an arbitrary operator perturbation.

Lemma 3.1 (Spectral response identity). Let \(\delta\Sigma\) be bounded and self-adjoint, and let \(V=\delta\Pi_k\) be the tangent vector it generates through the linear variation of the Riesz formula,

\[V=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma_k}(zI-\Sigma)^{-1}\,\delta\Sigma\,(zI-\Sigma)^{-1}\,dz. \tag{3.1}\]

Then, in an orthonormal eigenbasis \(\{|m\rangle\}\) of \(\Sigma\),

\[\langle m|V|n\rangle=\frac{\langle m|\delta\Sigma|n\rangle}{\lambda_{\rm in}-\lambda_{\rm out}}\quad\text{if exactly one of }m,n\text{ lies in }\mathcal I,\qquad \langle m|V|n\rangle=0\quad\text{otherwise}, \tag{3.2}\]

where \(\lambda_{\rm in}\) is the eigenvalue of the index inside \(\Gamma_k\) and \(\lambda_{\rm out}\) that of the index outside.

Proof. Sandwiching the integral between two eigenvectors and pulling the scalar denominators out of the inner product gives

\[\langle m|V|n\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma_k}\frac{\langle m|\delta\Sigma|n\rangle}{(z-\lambda_m)(z-\lambda_n)}\,dz .\]

The value is fixed by where the two poles sit relative to \(\Gamma_k\), which encloses precisely the eigenvalues of \(\Pi_k\). If both lie inside and \(\lambda_m\neq\lambda_n\), the residues \(1/(\lambda_m-\lambda_n)\) and \(1/(\lambda_n-\lambda_m)\) cancel; if \(\lambda_m=\lambda_n\) the pole is of order two and its residue vanishes. If both lie outside, the integrand is holomorphic inside \(\Gamma_k\) and the integral vanishes. If exactly one lies inside, its residue is \(1/(\lambda_{\rm in}-\lambda_{\rm out})\), which gives (3.2). ∎

Remark 3.2 (Degeneracy). Inside a degenerate block the eigenbasis is not unique, but (3.2) depends only on eigenvalues and on \(\Pi_k\), so it is unaffected by the choice. Equal eigenvalues necessarily lie on the same side of \(\Gamma_k\), so no cross-gap pair can have a vanishing denominator. This is precisely the work done by the gap hypothesis.

Remark 3.3 (Self-adjointness). For a cross-gap pair, \(\langle n|V|m\rangle=\langle n|\delta\Sigma|m\rangle/(\lambda_{\rm in}-\lambda_{\rm out})=\overline{\langle m|V|n\rangle}\), since the denominator is real. Hence \(V\) is self-adjoint, as a tangent vector must be.

Corollary 3.4 (Off-diagonal structure of tangent vectors). The first-order variation \(V=\delta\Pi_k\) possesses no matrix elements internal to either spectral sector: \(\Pi_kV\Pi_k=0\) and \((I-\Pi_k)V(I-\Pi_k)=0\). Consequently \(V\) has the block form (2.1), and the entire tangent geometry is generated by virtual transitions across the spectral gap.

Corollary 3.4 recovers by residue calculus what Section 2 obtained from the projector identity. The two statements are logically distinct: the earlier one covers all tangent vectors, this one those generated by a perturbation \(\delta\Sigma\). Every admissible tangent direction is generated exclusively by mixing between the spectral subspace of \(\Pi_k\) and its complement, and consequently both the metric and the curvature derived below depend on cross-gap transition amplitudes.

Proposition 3.5 (Gap bound). \(\displaystyle \|V\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\frac{\sqrt{2k}\,\|\delta\Sigma\|}{\Delta}.\)

Proof. From (2.1), \(\|V\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=2\|X\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\). By (3.2), in the eigenbasis \(X\) is \(\Pi\,\delta\Sigma\,(I-\Pi)\) divided entrywise by gaps \(\lambda_m-\lambda_n\) of modulus at least \(\Delta\). The Hilbert–Schmidt norm is the square root of the sum of the squared moduli of the entries, so \(\|X\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\sum_{m\in\mathcal I,\,n\in\mathcal I^c}|\langle m|\delta\Sigma|n\rangle|^2/(\lambda_m-\lambda_n)^2\le\|\Pi\,\delta\Sigma\,(I-\Pi)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2/\Delta^2\). The operator \(\Pi\,\delta\Sigma\,(I-\Pi)\) has rank at most \(k\) and operator norm at most \(\|\delta\Sigma\|\), so \(\|\Pi\,\delta\Sigma\,(I-\Pi)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\sqrt k\,\|\delta\Sigma\|\). Hence \(\|X\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\sqrt k\,\|\delta\Sigma\|/\Delta\), for any spectral set \(\mathcal I\), adjacent or not. ∎

The bound is sharp: a two-level system pushed by a purely off-diagonal \(\delta\Sigma\) attains it.

Theorem 3.6 (Spectral decomposition of the metric and curvature forms). Let \(V=\delta_1\Pi_k\) and \(W=\delta_2\Pi_k\) be generated by self-adjoint perturbations \(\delta_1\Sigma\) and \(\delta_2\Sigma\). Then

\[g_{\Pi_k}(V,W)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal I}\sum_{n\in\mathcal I^c}\frac{\mathrm{Re}\big[\langle m|\delta_1\Sigma|n\rangle\langle n|\delta_2\Sigma|m\rangle\big]}{(\lambda_n-\lambda_m)^2}, \tag{3.3}\] \[\Omega_{\Pi_k}(V,W)=i\sum_{m\in\mathcal I}\sum_{n\in\mathcal I^c}\frac{\langle m|\delta_1\Sigma|n\rangle\langle n|\delta_2\Sigma|m\rangle-\langle m|\delta_2\Sigma|n\rangle\langle n|\delta_1\Sigma|m\rangle}{(\lambda_n-\lambda_m)^2}. \tag{3.4}\]

In particular \(g_{\Pi_k}(V,V)=\sum_{m\in\mathcal I}\sum_{n\in\mathcal I^c}|\langle m|\delta_1\Sigma|n\rangle|^2/(\lambda_n-\lambda_m)^2\).

Proof of the symmetric metric component. From the block decomposition \(V=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\X^*&0\end{pmatrix}\), \(W=\begin{pmatrix}0&Y\\Y^*&0\end{pmatrix}\) we have \(g_{\Pi_k}(V,W)=\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Tr}(XY^*)=\mathrm{Re}\sum_{m\in\mathcal I,\,n\in\mathcal I^c}X_{mn}\overline{Y_{mn}}\). By (3.2) both entries carry the denominator \(\lambda_m-\lambda_n\), whose square is \((\lambda_n-\lambda_m)^2\), and \(\overline{\langle m|\delta_2\Sigma|n\rangle}=\langle n|\delta_2\Sigma|m\rangle\). ∎

Proof of the skew-symmetric curvature component. Inserting a resolution of the identity into \(\Omega_{\Pi_k}(V,W)=i\,\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_k[V,W])\) and restricting to cross-gap terms,

\[\Omega_{\Pi_k}(V,W)=i\sum_{m\in\mathcal I}\sum_{n\in\mathcal I^c}\Big(\langle m|V|n\rangle\langle n|W|m\rangle-\langle m|W|n\rangle\langle n|V|m\rangle\Big).\]

Each product carries the same denominator twice, \((\lambda_m-\lambda_n)(\lambda_m-\lambda_n)=(\lambda_n-\lambda_m)^2\), which gives (3.4). ∎

Remark 3.7 (The numerator is gap-weighted). The antisymmetric numerator in (3.4) is not the commutator \([\delta_1\Sigma,\delta_2\Sigma]\) projected across the gap: each term carries its own weight \((\lambda_n-\lambda_m)^{-2}\), and without the weights it would be. The Berry curvature measures the oriented phase accumulated by successive virtual transitions between the spectral subspace of \(\Pi_k\) and its complement, each suppressed by its own energy denominator.

Remark 3.8 (Beyond discrete spectrum). The operator statements — (2.1), Lemma 2.1, (2.3), \(g_\Pi(V,V)=\mathrm{Tr}(XX^*)\) and Proposition 3.5 — hold verbatim with no discreteness assumption. Only the explicit index sums (3.3)–(3.4) require it.

Remark 3.9 (Which perturbations generate which tangent vectors). In finite dimensions the map \(\delta\Sigma\mapsto V\) is onto \(T_\Pi\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\): given \(X\), take \(\langle m|\delta\Sigma|n\rangle=(\lambda_m-\lambda_n)X_{mn}\) on cross-gap pairs. In infinite dimensions this inverse may require an unbounded \(\delta\Sigma\), since the gaps are unbounded above; Lemma 2.1, which constructs a unitary flow directly, is unaffected and is the statement to use.
