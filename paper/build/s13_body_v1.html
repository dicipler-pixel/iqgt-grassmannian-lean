13 Conclusion

We have constructed a fully coordinate-free formulation of the Quantum Geometric Tensor on the Grassmannian manifold of rank-\(k\) spectral projectors associated with a gapped self-adjoint operator. By building directly on the Riesz–Kato spectral projection formalism, we have shown that the geometry of an isolated spectral subspace can be expressed entirely at the level of the orthogonal projector, without reference to local eigenvector bases, Berry connections, coordinate charts or parameter-space constructions.

The intrinsic tensor

\[Q_\Pi(V,W)=\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi VW)\]

provides a unified geometric object whose symmetric component is the Hilbert–Schmidt quantum metric and whose antisymmetric component is the Berry curvature 2-form. Both arise from the canonical Hermitian form on the Grassmannian tangent bundle. The fundamental tangent-space constraint makes explicit that all geometric response is generated exclusively by virtual cross-gap excitations between the occupied and complementary spectral subspaces.

The real and imaginary parts of the tensor are therefore intertwined rather than independent. The same cross-gap matrix elements that determine the quantum metric also control the Berry curvature, which yields the sharp metric–curvature inequality of Section 6. Under an explicit bath model the intrinsic metric, reweighted by the spectrum, governs the minimal dissipative cost of finite-time driving, and gives a lower bound on the excess work in terms of the intrinsic length of the path.

When applied to physical systems, the framework shows that parameter spaces do not generate geometric properties; they select finite-dimensional trajectories through a pre-existing Grassmannian geometry by pullback. This is why geometric transport is robust under smooth deformations of the Hamiltonian as long as the spectral gap stays open, and why, where the pullback degenerates, what happens next depends on whether the subspace has stopped rotating or the gap has narrowed.

By anchoring the construction in the functional-analytic foundations of analytic perturbation theory, the framework remains consistent across bounded matrix models and unbounded differential operators. It unifies perturbation theory, topological invariants, quantum thermodynamic geometry and the extraction of geometric angles from raw data within a single coordinate-free operator architecture.
