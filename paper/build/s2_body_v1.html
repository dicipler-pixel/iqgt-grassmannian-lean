2 Mathematical Framework and Intrinsic Geometric Structures

Let \(\mathcal H\) be a separable complex Hilbert space and let \(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\) denote the Grassmannian manifold of rank-\(k\) orthogonal projectors on \(\mathcal H\); it is a real-analytic Banach manifold. An element \(\Pi\in\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\) is a bounded self-adjoint operator satisfying

\[\Pi^\dagger=\Pi=\Pi^2,\qquad \mathrm{Tr}\,\Pi=k,\]

with \(k<\infty\) throughout.

2.1 The tangent space

The tangent space \(T_\Pi\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\) at a point \(\Pi\) consists of the self-adjoint operators \(V\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)\) satisfying the linearised constraint obtained by differentiating the projector identity \(\Pi^2=\Pi\). This yields the relation

\[V=\Pi V+V\Pi .\]

Multiplying on both sides by \(\Pi\) immediately gives \(\Pi V\Pi=0\). The same operation with the complementary projector forces \((I-\Pi)V(I-\Pi)=0\). Consequently, every tangent vector is strictly off-diagonal with respect to the orthogonal decomposition \(\mathcal H=\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi)\oplus\mathrm{Ran}(I-\Pi)\):

\[V=\Pi V(I-\Pi)+(I-\Pi)V\Pi=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\ X^{*}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad X=\Pi V(I-\Pi). \tag{2.1}\]

This structural constraint is fundamental: all geometric features of the tangent space arise exclusively from mixing between the occupied spectral subspace and its complement. There are no intra-subspace contributions to the geometry.

Lemma 2.1 (Exact characterisation of the tangent space). Let \(\Pi\in\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\) and let \(V\) be bounded, self-adjoint and of the form (2.1) for some bounded \(X:\mathrm{Ran}(I-\Pi)\to\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi)\). Then \(V\) is the velocity at \(t=0\) of a curve in \(\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\) through \(\Pi\). Consequently \(T_\Pi\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\) is exactly the set of bounded self-adjoint operators of the form (2.1).

Proof. Set

\[A=\begin{pmatrix}0&iX\\ -iX^{*}&0\end{pmatrix},\]

which is bounded and self-adjoint, and let \(\Pi(t)=e^{itA}\,\Pi\,e^{-itA}\). Each \(\Pi(t)\) is a rank-\(k\) orthogonal projector, and \(\dot\Pi(0)=i[A,\Pi]=V\). In finite dimensions the dimension count agrees: an arbitrary complex \(X\) of size \(k\times(n-k)\) has real dimension \(2k(n-k)=\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb C^n)\). ∎

Remark 2.2 (Convergence is automatic). \(X=\Pi V(I-\Pi)\) factors through \(\mathrm{Ran}(\Pi)\) and therefore has rank at most \(k\). A bounded finite-rank operator is trace class, so every trace appearing below converges for any bounded \(V\), with no additional hypothesis. This is the only place finiteness of \(k\) is used.

2.2 The Intrinsic Quantum Geometric Tensor

The tangent space is a real vector space: if \(V\) is self-adjoint, \(iV\) is not. We therefore define the intrinsic Quantum Geometric Tensor, the canonical Hermitian form on the Grassmannian, on the complexification \(T^{\mathbb C}_\Pi=\{A+iB:\ A,B\in T_\Pi\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\}\):

\[Q_\Pi(A,B):=\mathrm{Tr}\big(\Pi A^{\dagger}B\big). \tag{2.2}\]

This form is sesquilinear, reduces to \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi VW)\) on self-adjoint arguments, and is positive semi-definite:

\[Q_\Pi(A,A)=\mathrm{Tr}\big(\Pi A^{\dagger}A\,\Pi\big)=\|A\Pi\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^{2}\ \ge 0 .\]

On the real tangent space it is definite, since there the two off-diagonal blocks are adjoint to one another; on the complexification it is only semi-definite, because a complex tangent vector carrying only its upper block has \(A\Pi=0\).

For \(V,W\in T_\Pi\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\) with blocks \(X,Y\) one computes \(VW=\mathrm{diag}(XY^{*},X^{*}Y)\), hence \(Q_\Pi(V,W)=\mathrm{Tr}(XY^{*})\). This complex-valued form decomposes uniquely into a symmetric real part and a skew-symmetric imaginary part,

\[Q_\Pi(V,W)=g_\Pi(V,W)-\tfrac{i}{2}\,\Omega_\Pi(V,W), \tag{2.3}\] \[g_\Pi(V,W)=\tfrac12\,\mathrm{Tr}(VW)=\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{Tr}(XY^{*}),\qquad \Omega_\Pi(V,W)=i\,\mathrm{Tr}\big(\Pi[V,W]\big)=-2\,\mathrm{Im}\,\mathrm{Tr}(XY^{*}),\]

where \(g_\Pi\) is the intrinsic Hilbert–Schmidt Riemannian metric and \(\Omega_\Pi\) is the intrinsic Berry curvature 2-form. These are the signs of the standard parametric convention \(Q=g-\tfrac{i}{2}F\): pulled back along a family of projectors, \(\Omega_\Pi\) is the ordinary Berry curvature \(F=dA\), \(A=i\langle u|du\rangle\), for \(k=1\), and the trace of the non-abelian curvature for \(k>1\). The metric \(g_\Pi\) quantifies the infinitesimal distinguishability between nearby projectors, while the curvature \(\Omega_\Pi\) encodes the geometric phase accumulated along closed paths in the Grassmannian. Both quantities derive from the same underlying form and are therefore linked through the off-diagonal structure of the tangent space.

Remark 2.3 (Where the \(\Pi\)-dependence lives). The metric \(g_\Pi(V,W)=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}(VW)\) appears to have forgotten \(\Pi\). It has not. The identity \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi VW)+\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi WV)=\mathrm{Tr}(VW)\) is false for general operators; it holds precisely because \(V\) and \(W\) are off-diagonal with respect to this \(\Pi\). The dependence has migrated from the formula into its domain.

Remark 2.4 (The factor \(\tfrac12\) is forced). On the Bloch sphere, \(\Pi_-=\tfrac12(I-\hat n\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)\) gives \(\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}(V^2)=\tfrac14|\delta\hat n|^2\), hence \(g_{\theta\theta}=1/4\): the Fubini–Study value. The alternative normalisation \(\mathrm{Tr}(VW)\) would give \(1/2\) and disagree with the parametric literature. The factor is fixed by that agreement, not chosen.

2.3 Riesz–Kato Spectral Projectors

Let \(\Sigma\) be a self-adjoint operator (bounded or unbounded) possessing an isolated spectral subspace of dimension \(k\) separated from the remainder of the spectrum by a positive gap \(\Delta>0\). Let \(\Gamma_k\) be a simple closed contour lying in the resolvent set that encloses precisely the eigenvalues associated with this subspace. Write \(\mathcal I\) for the set of spectral indices inside \(\Gamma_k\) and \(\mathcal I^{c}\) for those outside. The corresponding spectral projector is given by the Riesz integral

\[\Pi_k=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\Gamma_k}(zI-\Sigma)^{-1}\,dz. \tag{2.4}\]

This projector is independent of the particular choice of contour within the gap and depends analytically on \(\Sigma\) as long as the gap remains open; a contour that crosses the gap encloses a different spectral subspace and returns a different projector. Appendix A treats the unbounded case. All subsequent geometric constructions are formulated directly in terms of \(\Pi_k\) and its tangent variations.
