6 Metric–Curvature Inequalities, Equality and Rigidity

The preceding sections established that the intrinsic Quantum Geometric Tensor admits a canonical decomposition into its symmetric metric component and antisymmetric curvature component, \(Q_\Pi=g_\Pi-\tfrac i2\Omega_\Pi\). While these objects are traditionally presented as independent geometric quantities in parametric band theory, the coordinate-free projector formulation reveals a structural constraint: the generation of a geometric phase is quantitatively bounded by the intrinsic metric of the Grassmannian tangent bundle. In coordinates the bound below is the determinant inequality \(4\det g\ge\Omega^2\), standard in the band-geometry literature [Roy 2014; Mera–Ozawa 2021]; the projector formulation makes both its proof and its saturation transparent, and no priority is claimed for it.

Theorem 6.1 (Metric control of Berry curvature). Let \(V,W\in T_\Pi\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\). The intrinsic Berry curvature 2-form satisfies the sharp inequality

\[|\Omega_\Pi(V,W)|\ \le\ 2\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)\,g_\Pi(W,W)-g_\Pi(V,W)^2}. \tag{6.1}\]

Proof. By (2.2), \(Q_\Pi\) is a positive semi-definite Hermitian form on the complexified tangent space. Consequently, for any two tangent vectors the \(2\times2\) Gram matrix of their inner products has non-negative determinant:

\[\det\begin{pmatrix}Q_\Pi(V,V)&Q_\Pi(V,W)\\ Q_\Pi(W,V)&Q_\Pi(W,W)\end{pmatrix}=Q_\Pi(V,V)\,Q_\Pi(W,W)-|Q_\Pi(V,W)|^2\ \ge\ 0 .\]

Using the off-diagonal constraint \(\Pi V\Pi=0\), \(Q_\Pi(V,V)=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}(V^2)=g_\Pi(V,V)\), and likewise for \(W\). Separating the cross term into its real and imaginary parts by (2.3), \(|Q_\Pi(V,W)|^2=g_\Pi(V,W)^2+\tfrac14\Omega_\Pi(V,W)^2\). Substituting, \(g_\Pi(V,V)g_\Pi(W,W)-g_\Pi(V,W)^2-\tfrac14\Omega_\Pi(V,W)^2\ge0\); rearranging and taking the square root completes the proof. ∎

Dropping the subtracted term gives the weaker envelope \(|\Omega_\Pi(V,W)|\le2\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)\,g_\Pi(W,W)}\), which also follows directly from the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz applied to \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi VW)\) and \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi WV)\).

Corollary 6.2 (Sharpness, with an explicit extremal pair). Equality holds in (6.1) if and only if \(Y=cX\) for some \(c\in\mathbb C\), where \(X,Y\) are the blocks of \(V,W\). In particular, given any \(V\ne0\) with block \(X\), the tangent vector \(W\) whose block is \(iX\) (which exists by Lemma 2.1) satisfies \(g_\Pi(V,W)=0\) and \(|\Omega_\Pi(V,W)|=2\|X\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=2\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)g_\Pi(W,W)}\), so the bound is attained, in every rank \(k\).

Proof. Equality in the Cauchy–Schwarz step holds exactly when \(X\) and \(Y\) are linearly dependent over \(\mathbb C\). Writing \(Y=cX\) gives \(g_\Pi(V,V)=\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\), \(g_\Pi(W,W)=|c|^2\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\), \(g_\Pi(V,W)=\mathrm{Re}(\bar c)\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\) and \(\Omega_\Pi(V,W)=-2\,\mathrm{Im}(\bar c)\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\); both sides of (6.1) equal \(2|\mathrm{Im}\,c|\,\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\). Setting \(c=i\) gives the stated pair. ∎

In coordinate form, saturation of the determinant inequality is known to occur exactly when the parametric tensor acquires a null vector, and bands saturating it everywhere are called Kähler, ideal or vortexable. Corollary 6.2 is that criterion in block language: \(Y=cX\) is the statement that the Gram matrix of \(V,W\) is singular, and the extremal partner can be written down explicitly, in every rank, as the tangent vector whose block is \(iX\). Pulled back along a two-parameter family, (6.1) is \(4\det g_{ij}\ge\Omega_{12}^2\).

6.1 The geometric angle safeguard

Expressing the metric inner product as \(g_\Pi(V,W)=\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)g_\Pi(W,W)}\,\cos\theta_G\), where \(\theta_G\) is the geometric angle between the two tangent deformations, turns (6.1) into

\[|\Omega_\Pi(V,W)|\ \le\ 2\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)\,g_\Pi(W,W)}\;|\sin\theta_G| . \tag{6.2}\]

This \(\sin\theta_G\) dependence is a directional safeguard: the curvature a pair of deformations can generate is limited by how far apart they point in the intrinsic metric. The control it gives is relative, not absolute. Near a degeneracy both sides of (6.2) diverge at the same rate — a two-level system saturates the bound at every gap, so the ratio stays one as the gap closes — and the inequality is not a regularisation. The suppression at \(\sin\theta_G=0\) is a property of antisymmetry, since any 2-form vanishes on proportional arguments.

6.2 Control hierarchy

Both geometric quantities are driven by the same interband transition amplitudes \(\langle m|\delta\Sigma|n\rangle\) across the spectral cut (Theorem 3.6). The quantum metric quantifies the total capacity of the transport channels; the Berry curvature tracks the phase interference among them; and the metric is the quantitative envelope for every admissible curvature. Metric rigidity therefore enforces curvature rigidity: if \(g_\Pi(V,V)=0\) then \(V=0\) and \(\Omega_\Pi(V,\cdot)=0\), and if a perturbation has no cross-gap coupling, \(\langle m|\delta\Sigma|n\rangle=0\) for every \(m\in\mathcal I,\ n\in\mathcal I^c\), then it moves nothing and carries no curvature. The ordering is one of control, not of generation: cross-gap transitions generate both \(g\) and \(\Omega\), and \(g\) bounds \(\Omega\).

6.3 When the bounds are met

Every inequality in this paper is sharp, and in every case the equality is rigid: it holds only in one recognisable configuration, which equality alone forces. This subsection collects the equality cases. Each is proved from the proof of its inequality, by asking where each step loses nothing.

Theorem 6.3 (Equality cases). Let \(B=\Pi\,\delta\Sigma\,(I-\Pi)\).

  1. Gap bound. \(\|V\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt{2k}\,\|\delta\Sigma\|/\Delta\) in Proposition 3.5 if and only if (a) \(\delta\Sigma\) couples the two sectors only through pairs \((m,n)\) with \(|\lambda_m-\lambda_n|=\Delta\) exactly, and (b) \(B\) has \(k\) nonzero singular values, all equal to \(\|\delta\Sigma\|\).
  2. Minimum driving time. Theorem 5.2 holds with equality if and only if, at almost every time, the gap bound holds with equality at the gap \(\Delta(t)=\Delta_{\min}\), with drive strength \(\|\partial_t\Sigma(t)\|=\sup_s\|\partial_s\Sigma(s)\|\).
  3. Dissipation. The chain (5.5) holds with equality if and only if \(\zeta=\zeta_{\rm rot}\) (no part of the drive moves the Gibbs state without moving \(\Pi\)), the weight \(2c(\Delta_n)\Delta_n\) equals its minimum over all pairs and times on every pair that carries metric, at every time, and the intrinsic speed \(g_\Pi(\dot\Pi,\dot\Pi)\) is constant in time.
  4. Curvature. Equality in (6.1) holds if and only if \(W\) lies in the complex line of \(V\) (Corollary 6.2). Quantitatively, for \(V\ne0\), \[g_\Pi(V,V)\,g_\Pi(W,W)-g_\Pi(V,W)^2-\tfrac14\Omega_\Pi(V,W)^2=g_\Pi(V,V)\;g_\Pi(W_\perp,W_\perp), \tag{6.3}\] where \(W_\perp\) is the tangent vector with block \(Y-cX\), \(c=\mathrm{Tr}(YX^*)/\mathrm{Tr}(XX^*)\).

Proof. (i) The proof of Proposition 3.5 is a chain of three steps: \(\sum|B_{mn}|^2/(\lambda_m-\lambda_n)^2\le\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2/\Delta^2\); \(\|B\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\sqrt k\,\|B\|\); and \(\|B\|\le\|\delta\Sigma\|\). The first is a sum of termwise inequalities, each an equality exactly when \(B_{mn}=0\) or \(|\lambda_m-\lambda_n|=\Delta\). The second compares the root sum of the squares of at most \(k\) singular values with \(\sqrt k\) times the largest, with equality exactly when all \(k\) are equal. The third is then an equality exactly when that common value is \(\|\delta\Sigma\|\). (ii) The proof of Theorem 5.2 bounds the integrand pointwise by (i) and then replaces \(\Delta(t)\) by \(\Delta_{\min}\) and \(\|\partial_t\Sigma\|\) by its supremum; the integral equals the bound exactly when each replacement loses nothing almost everywhere. (iii) The three inequalities of (5.5) are \(\zeta\ge\zeta_{\rm rot}\), the convex-combination bound (5.4), and Cauchy–Schwarz \(\mathcal L^2\le T\int g\,dt\), which is an equality exactly when \(\sqrt g\) is constant. (iv) With \(g_\Pi(V,V)=\|X\|^2\), \(g_\Pi(W,W)=\|Y\|^2\) and \(g_\Pi(V,W)^2+\tfrac14\Omega_\Pi(V,W)^2=|Q_\Pi(V,W)|^2=|\mathrm{Tr}(XY^*)|^2\), the left side of (6.3) is \(\|X\|^2\|Y\|^2-|\mathrm{Tr}(XY^*)|^2\). Writing \(Y=cX+(Y-cX)\) with the two parts orthogonal, \(\|Y\|^2=|c|^2\|X\|^2+\|Y-cX\|^2\) and \(|\mathrm{Tr}(XY^*)|^2=|c|^2\|X\|^4\), which gives (6.3). ∎

Each equality case is a picture. In (i) the drive acts as \(k\) couplings of one strength across one gap; the simplest instance is \(k\) copies of a two-level system, and in the checks of this subsection that configuration meets the bound to \(10^{-8}\) for \(k=1,2,3\). Moving one coupled partner from the gap \(\Delta\) to \(1.5\Delta\) drops the ratio to 0.67–0.90, and unequal coupling strengths drop it to 0.79–0.87. In (ii) the equality is a rigid rotation: \(\Sigma(t)=\tfrac\Delta2\,\mathbf n(t)\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\) with \(\mathbf n\) moving along a great circle at constant rate meets Theorem 5.2 exactly (ratio \(1.000000\)), while the same rotation with a breathing gap reaches only \(0.667\). In (iii), with one gap and zero temperature, \(\int g\,dt=\mathcal L^2/T\) exactly at constant speed (ratio \(1.00000000\)), and a speed modulated by 60% overspends by 19%. The cheapest protocol therefore moves the subspace along a geodesic at constant intrinsic speed, the thermodynamic-length result of Salamon and Berry in this setting [Salamon–Berry 1983].

Equation (6.3) says more than the equality case. For fixed \(V\), the deficit of the curvature bound is a nonnegative quadratic form in \(W\), and its kernel is exactly the plane spanned by \(V\) and its partner \(JV\), the tangent vector with block \(iX\). The map \(J:X\mapsto iX\) is the complex structure of the Grassmannian, so equality and rigidity are one statement: two directions saturate the bound exactly when they span a complex line. (Identity (6.3) holds on 300 random pairs to \(10^{-6}\) relative.)

Proposition 6.4 (The trace condition and its equality). For a two-parameter family and any Riemannian metric \(h\) on its parameter surface,

\[\mathrm{tr}_h\,g\ \ge\ 2\sqrt{\det g/\det h}\ \ge\ \frac{|\Omega_{12}|}{\sqrt{\det h}} . \tag{6.4}\]

The first inequality is an equality exactly when \(g\) is conformal to \(h\); the second exactly when the curvature bound is saturated.

Proof. If \(\mu_1,\mu_2\ge0\) are the eigenvalues of \(h^{-1}g\), then \(\mathrm{tr}_hg=\mu_1+\mu_2\ge2\sqrt{\mu_1\mu_2}=2\sqrt{\det g/\det h}\), with equality exactly when \(\mu_1=\mu_2\), that is \(g=\mu h\). The second inequality is \(4\det g\ge\Omega_{12}^2\), the pullback of (6.1). ∎

With \(h\) Euclidean on a Brillouin zone, (6.4) is the trace condition \(\mathrm{tr}\,g\ge|\Omega|\) of ideal bands [Roy 2014]. With \(h\) the round metric of the parameter sphere, both bands of Appendices B and C meet it with equality at every point: the two-level band has \(g=\tfrac14h\) and \(|\Omega_{\theta\phi}|=\tfrac12\sin\theta\), the lowest spin-1 band \(g=\tfrac12h\) and \(|\Omega_{\theta\phi}|=\sin\theta\) (both to \(6\times10^{-16}\)). Breaking the spin-1 symmetry with \(0.8\,S_z^2\) lifts it strictly above, by 0.04 to 0.46 over the sample. The same equality holds for the lowest band in every spin \(j\), which is the lowest Landau level on a sphere (Appendix C). Equality in both steps means the map is conformal and its image is a complex line at every point, which is what a holomorphic band is.

Example 6.5 (A loop that comes back still remembers). Carry the lower two-level band once around the circle of polar angle \(\theta_0=60^\circ\). The projector returns exactly to where it started (to \(10^{-16}\)), so no measurement of the final subspace can tell that the loop happened. The state vector does not return: it comes back multiplied by the Berry phase \(-\tfrac12\times\)(enclosed solid angle) \(=-\pi(1-\cos\theta_0)=-\pi/2\), computed from overlaps around the loop as \(-1.570797\). The phase is the curvature of this section integrated over the cap, \(\iint\Omega\). The subspace is back where it began; the history of the path is kept only in the phase, and only the curvature can write it there.
