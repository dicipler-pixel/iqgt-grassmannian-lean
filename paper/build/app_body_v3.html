A Unbounded Operators: Relative Boundedness and Domain Stabilisation

Let \(\Sigma\) be a (possibly unbounded) self-adjoint operator on the separable Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\) with dense domain \(D(\Sigma)\). For the Riesz projector \(\Pi_k\) of (2.4) to be well defined, the contour \(\Gamma_k\) must lie entirely in the resolvent set \(\rho(\Sigma)\).

When \(\Sigma\) is unbounded, stability under perturbations requires the notion of relative boundedness. An operator \(B\) is relatively bounded with respect to \(\Sigma\) if there exist constants \(a,b\ge0\) such that

\[\|B\psi\|\le a\|\Sigma\psi\|+b\|\psi\|\qquad\text{for all }\psi\in D(\Sigma). \tag{A.1}\]

The constant \(a\) is the relative bound of \(B\) with respect to \(\Sigma\).

The fundamental tool for controlling perturbations is the second resolvent identity. With \(A=zI-\Sigma\) and \(B=zI-\Sigma-\delta\Sigma\), the algebraic identity \(A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}\) and \(B-A=-\delta\Sigma\) give

\[(zI-\Sigma)^{-1}-(zI-\Sigma-\delta\Sigma)^{-1}=-(zI-\Sigma)^{-1}\,\delta\Sigma\,(zI-\Sigma-\delta\Sigma)^{-1}. \tag{A.2}\]

Solving (A.2) for the perturbed resolvent gives the Neumann series \((zI-\Sigma-\delta\Sigma)^{-1}=(zI-\Sigma)^{-1}\sum_{n\ge0}\big(\delta\Sigma(zI-\Sigma)^{-1}\big)^n\), which converges uniformly on the contour under the condition

\[\sup_{z\in\Gamma_k}\big\|\delta\Sigma\,(zI-\Sigma)^{-1}\big\|<1 . \tag{A.3}\]

Because \(\Gamma_k\) runs through the gap, (A.3) asks that the perturbation be small compared with the gap, not merely with the distance to far-away spectrum. For a relatively bounded \(\delta\Sigma\) it holds whenever \(a\sup_{z\in\Gamma_k,\lambda\in\sigma(\Sigma)}|\lambda/(z-\lambda)|+b/\mathrm{dist}(\Gamma_k,\sigma(\Sigma))<1\). Under (A.3) the perturbed resolvent exists on \(\Gamma_k\), the spectral gap persists, and \(\Pi_k\) depends analytically on the perturbation. In a six-level test with \(\sup_{\Gamma}\|\delta\Sigma(zI-\Sigma)^{-1}\|=0.44\), the perturbed projector computed by contour quadrature has trace \(2.00000000\); the identity (A.2) holds to \(2\times10^{-16}\), and the same identity with a plus sign fails by \(0.26\).

Remark A.1 (Rank is preserved). That \(\Gamma_k\) continues to enclose the same number of eigenvalues follows because \(\mathrm{Tr}\,\Pi_k\) is continuous along the perturbation and integer-valued, hence locally constant. The projector itself is bounded whatever \(\Sigma\) is; no domain question arises for it.

Proposition A.2 (Gap bound, unbounded case). Let \(\delta\Sigma\) be self-adjoint and relatively bounded with constants \(a,b\). Then

\[\|V\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\frac{\sqrt{2k}\,\big(a\max_{m\in\mathcal I}|\lambda_m|+b\big)}{\Delta}. \tag{A.4}\]

Proof. \(\mathrm{Ran}\,\Pi_k\) is spanned by eigenvectors with eigenvalues in a compact set, so \(\|\delta\Sigma\,\Pi_k\psi\|\le a\|\Sigma\Pi_k\psi\|+b\|\Pi_k\psi\|\le(a\max_{m\in\mathcal I}|\lambda_m|+b)\|\psi\|\). Since \(\delta\Sigma\) is self-adjoint, \(\|\Pi_k\delta\Sigma(I-\Pi_k)\|=\|(I-\Pi_k)\delta\Sigma\,\Pi_k\|\le\|\delta\Sigma\,\Pi_k\|\). Now argue as in Proposition 3.5. ∎

On 500 random instances with \(\delta\Sigma=a\Sigma+bB\), \(\|B\|\le1\), the left side of (A.4) never exceeded \(0.39\) of the right. Relative boundedness alone therefore suffices: the framework extends to unbounded \(\Sigma\) with an explicit bound. The hypothesis holds for a wide class of physically relevant perturbations, including vector potentials entering differential operators and many-body interaction terms; the standard instance is the Kato–Rellich theorem, by which the Coulomb potential is relatively bounded with respect to the Laplacian with relative bound zero [Kato 1995; Reed–Simon 1975].

B Two-Band Model: Explicit Mapping and Singular Angular Stability

Consider the canonical two-band Dirac operator parameterised by a vector \(\mathbf d=(d_x,d_y,d_z)\),

\[\Sigma(\mathbf d)=\mathbf d\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma ,\]

with eigenvalues \(\lambda_\pm=\pm|\mathbf d|\) and gap \(\Delta=2|\mathbf d|\). The rank-one projector onto the lower band is

\[\Pi_-=\tfrac12\big(I-\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma\big),\qquad \hat{\mathbf d}=\mathbf d/|\mathbf d| ,\]

so \(\Pi_-\) depends on \(\mathbf d\) only through its direction on the Bloch sphere.

Let \(V=\delta_1\Pi_-\) and \(W=\delta_2\Pi_-\) be generated by variations \(\delta_1\mathbf d\), \(\delta_2\mathbf d\), and write \(\delta\mathbf d_\perp=\delta\mathbf d-(\delta\mathbf d\cdot\hat{\mathbf d})\hat{\mathbf d}\) for the part tangent to the sphere. The intrinsic metric and curvature are

\[g_{\Pi_-}(V,W)=\frac{\delta_1\mathbf d_\perp\cdot\delta_2\mathbf d_\perp}{4|\mathbf d|^2},\qquad \Omega_{\Pi_-}(V,W)=\frac{\mathbf d\cdot(\delta_1\mathbf d\times\delta_2\mathbf d)}{2|\mathbf d|^3}. \tag{B.1}\]

Both forms agree with the trace definitions (2.3) on 200 random configurations to \(2\times10^{-10}\), with the sign convention of Section 2.

Substituting (B.1) into Theorem 6.1, the subtraction term inside the radical becomes, by \(|\mathbf A|^2|\mathbf B|^2-(\mathbf A\cdot\mathbf B)^2=|\mathbf A\times\mathbf B|^2\),

\[g_{\Pi_-}(V,V)\,g_{\Pi_-}(W,W)-g_{\Pi_-}(V,W)^2=\frac{|\delta_1\mathbf d_\perp\times\delta_2\mathbf d_\perp|^2}{16|\mathbf d|^4},\]

so that \(2\sqrt{gg-g^2}=|\delta_1\mathbf d_\perp\times\delta_2\mathbf d_\perp|/(2|\mathbf d|^2)\). Because \(\mathbf d\cdot(\delta_1\mathbf d\times\delta_2\mathbf d)=|\mathbf d|\,\hat{\mathbf d}\cdot(\delta_1\mathbf d_\perp\times\delta_2\mathbf d_\perp)\) and the cross product of two tangent vectors is normal to the sphere, the curvature has exactly the same modulus:

\[|\Omega_{\Pi_-}(V,W)|=2\sqrt{g_{\Pi_-}(V,V)\,g_{\Pi_-}(W,W)-g_{\Pi_-}(V,W)^2}. \tag{B.2}\]

The bound is saturated for every pair of directions (to \(3\times10^{-11}\) on 200 random pairs). This saturation is forced: with \(k=1\) and a one-dimensional complement, the blocks \(X\) and \(Y\) of (2.1) are complex numbers, \(|Q_\Pi(V,W)|^2=|X|^2|Y|^2=Q_\Pi(V,V)Q_\Pi(W,W)\), and the Gram determinant in the proof of Theorem 6.1 vanishes identically. Every two-band pair is an extremal partner in the sense of Corollary 6.2.

Introducing the angle \(\theta\) between the tangent variations, \(|\delta_1\mathbf d_\perp\times\delta_2\mathbf d_\perp|=|\delta_1\mathbf d_\perp||\delta_2\mathbf d_\perp|\sin\theta\), exposes the directional safeguard. As the node \(|\mathbf d|\to0\) is approached, the metric components grow as \(|\mathbf d|^{-2}\) and the curvature grows at the same rate, so the curvature per unit of metric stays fixed by \(\sin\theta\): control is relative, as in Section 6.1. For collinear variations (\(\theta=0\)) the curvature vanishes identically however close the node is.

In spherical coordinates \((\theta,\phi)\) on the Bloch sphere the components reduce to the monopole values

\[g_{\theta\theta}=\tfrac14,\qquad g_{\phi\phi}=\tfrac14\sin^2\theta,\qquad \Omega_{\theta\phi}=\tfrac12\sin\theta, \tag{B.3}\]

and the first Chern number of the lower band is \(\frac1{2\pi}\int_{S^2}\Omega_{\theta\phi}\,d\theta\,d\phi=1\) (quadrature: \(1.0000026\)). The coordinate-free construction therefore recovers the conventional parametric geometry and its topological integer exactly.

C Three-Band Model: Flat-Band Projections and Particle–Hole Duality

Consider the spin-1 operator

\[\Sigma(\mathbf d)=\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf S ,\]

with eigenvalues \(\lambda_\pm=\pm|\mathbf d|\) and a flat middle band \(\lambda_0=0\). For the rank-one projector \(\Pi_1\) onto the lowest band and the rank-two projector \(\Pi_2\) onto the lowest two bands, explicit computation gives identical intrinsic metrics:

\[g_{\Pi_1}(V,V)=g_{\Pi_2}(V,V)=\frac{|\delta\mathbf d_\perp|^2}{2|\mathbf d|^2}. \tag{C.1}\]

The coefficient \(\tfrac12\) holds on 100 random configurations to \(6\times10^{-9}\) for both ranks.

This establishes an exact particle–hole duality on the Grassmannian, and its mechanism has two parts. The first holds for every projector: \(g_\Pi=g_{I-\Pi}\), because \(-V\) is the tangent vector of \(I-\Pi\) and the metric is quadratic (Section 2; on 100 random rank-two projectors of size six with no flat band, the two agree to \(10^{-12}\)). Hence \(g_{\Pi_2}=g_{\Pi_+}\), the metric of the top band. The second part is a property of the spin-1 spectrum: \(\Pi_+(\mathbf d)=\Pi_1(-\mathbf d)\), so the top and bottom bands carry the same geometry. Together they give (C.1). The second part is what the flat, symmetric middle band supplies: adding a term \(0.8\,S_z^2\), which breaks the \(\pm|\mathbf d|\) symmetry, separates the two metrics (their ratio ranges from \(0.11\) to \(11\) over 50 configurations), while \(g_\Pi=g_{I-\Pi}\) continues to hold exactly.

The lowest spin-1 band also saturates Theorem 6.1, exactly as the two-band model does (to \(2\times10^{-12}\) on 200 random pairs). Saturation is not a two-band accident, but it is not generic either: for a three-band family \(\Sigma=H_0+p_1H_1+p_2H_2\) with random Hermitian \(H_i\), the lowest band falls strictly inside the bound, with \(|\Omega|\) as small as one five-hundredth of the bound (Figure 17). Why the spin-1 band saturates is not derived here.

D Numerical Benchmarking on Lattice Models and Krylov Methods

To validate the coordinate-free framework on lattice systems, the geometric construction is implemented for a two-dimensional tight-binding Hamiltonian \(H_L(\mathbf a)\) with Peierls phases controlled by parameters \(\mathbf a\). The spectral projector onto the lowest \(k\) bands is computed by trapezoidal quadrature of the Riesz integral over a contour \(\Gamma_k\) in the gap,

\[\Pi_k\approx\frac1{2\pi i}\sum_j w_j\,(z_jI-H_L)^{-1}, \tag{D.1}\]

which converges exponentially in the number of nodes on a circle that stays away from the spectrum. The tangent vectors are obtained by differentiating the Riesz resolvent, \(V_i=\partial_{a_i}\Pi_k\), and the gauge-invariant metric and curvature markers are

\[g_{ij}=\tfrac12\,\mathrm{Tr}(V_iV_j),\qquad \Omega_{ij}=i\,\mathrm{Tr}\big(\Pi_k[V_i,V_j]\big), \tag{D.2}\]

with the sign of Section 2. Along a finite-time protocol the excess work is the Riemann sum \(W_{\mathrm{ex}}\approx\sum_m\Delta t\,\zeta(\dot{\mathbf a}_m)\) of the friction functional of Section 5.3, which by Theorem 5.3 is bounded below by the band-weighted intrinsic metric.

For large sparse lattices, full diagonalisation and dense inversion are bypassed by a Krylov iteration. Let \(|\psi_{m,i}\rangle=\partial_{a_i}H_L|m\rangle\) for each occupied state \(|m\rangle\), \(m\in\mathcal I\). The regularised cross-gap response vector solves the shifted system

\[\big[(H_L-\lambda_mI)^2+\eta^2I\big]\,|X^\eta_{m,i}\rangle=(I-\Pi_k)|\psi_{m,i}\rangle . \tag{D.3}\]

The operator on the left is Hermitian and positive definite, so conjugate gradients (or MINRES) is the solver to use. The same solutions give both halves of the tensor:

\[g^\eta_{ij}=\sum_{m\in\mathcal I}\mathrm{Re}\,\langle\psi_{m,i}|X^\eta_{m,j}\rangle,\qquad \Omega^\eta_{ij}=-2\sum_{m\in\mathcal I}\mathrm{Im}\,\langle\psi_{m,i}|X^\eta_{m,j}\rangle , \tag{D.4}\]

which reduce to the spectral sums (3.3)–(3.4) as \(\eta\to0\). The error is quadratic in the regulator. On a \(14\times14\) lattice (196 sites, \(k=3\)) the relative error against the exact pair sums is \(8.3\times10^{-4}\), \(8.3\times10^{-6}\) and \(8.3\times10^{-12}\) for the metric, and \(1.4\times10^{-3}\), \(1.4\times10^{-5}\) and \(1.4\times10^{-11}\) for the curvature, at \(\eta=10^{-2},10^{-3},10^{-6}\) (Figure 18), with 153 conjugate-gradient iterations per solve. The cost is \(k\) solves per parameter direction, each of order (non-zeros)\(\times\)(iterations): linear in the occupied rank \(k\), and still growing with the lattice through the number of non-zeros. Higher-order tensors such as the quantum metric dipole are obtained either by differentiating \(g^\eta_{ij}\) along \(\mathbf a\) or from the exact second-order formula (7.3).
