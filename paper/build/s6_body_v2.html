6 Metric–Curvature Inequalities and Geometric Control

The preceding sections established that the intrinsic Quantum Geometric Tensor admits a canonical decomposition into its symmetric metric component and antisymmetric curvature component, \(Q_\Pi=g_\Pi-\tfrac i2\Omega_\Pi\). While these objects are traditionally presented as independent geometric quantities in parametric band theory, the coordinate-free projector formulation reveals a structural constraint: the generation of a geometric phase is quantitatively bounded by the intrinsic metric of the Grassmannian tangent bundle. In coordinates the bound below is the determinant inequality \(4\det g\ge\Omega^2\), standard in the band-geometry literature [Roy 2014; Mera–Ozawa 2021]; the projector formulation makes both its proof and its saturation transparent, and no priority is claimed for it.

Theorem 6.1 (Metric control of Berry curvature). Let \(V,W\in T_\Pi\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathcal H)\). The intrinsic Berry curvature 2-form satisfies the sharp inequality

\[|\Omega_\Pi(V,W)|\ \le\ 2\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)\,g_\Pi(W,W)-g_\Pi(V,W)^2}. \tag{6.1}\]

Proof. By (2.2), \(Q_\Pi\) is a positive semi-definite Hermitian form on the complexified tangent space. Consequently, for any two tangent vectors the \(2\times2\) Gram matrix of their inner products has non-negative determinant:

\[\det\begin{pmatrix}Q_\Pi(V,V)&Q_\Pi(V,W)\\ Q_\Pi(W,V)&Q_\Pi(W,W)\end{pmatrix}=Q_\Pi(V,V)\,Q_\Pi(W,W)-|Q_\Pi(V,W)|^2\ \ge\ 0 .\]

Using the off-diagonal constraint \(\Pi V\Pi=0\), \(Q_\Pi(V,V)=\tfrac12\mathrm{Tr}(V^2)=g_\Pi(V,V)\), and likewise for \(W\). Separating the cross term into its real and imaginary parts by (2.3), \(|Q_\Pi(V,W)|^2=g_\Pi(V,W)^2+\tfrac14\Omega_\Pi(V,W)^2\). Substituting, \(g_\Pi(V,V)g_\Pi(W,W)-g_\Pi(V,W)^2-\tfrac14\Omega_\Pi(V,W)^2\ge0\); rearranging and taking the square root completes the proof. ∎

Dropping the subtracted term gives the weaker envelope \(|\Omega_\Pi(V,W)|\le2\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)\,g_\Pi(W,W)}\), which also follows directly from the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz applied to \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi VW)\) and \(\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi WV)\).

Corollary 6.2 (Sharpness, with an explicit extremal pair). Equality holds in (6.1) if and only if \(Y=cX\) for some \(c\in\mathbb C\), where \(X,Y\) are the blocks of \(V,W\). In particular, given any \(V\ne0\) with block \(X\), the tangent vector \(W\) whose block is \(iX\) (which exists by Lemma 2.1) satisfies \(g_\Pi(V,W)=0\) and \(|\Omega_\Pi(V,W)|=2\|X\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=2\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)g_\Pi(W,W)}\), so the bound is attained, in every rank \(k\).

Proof. Equality in the Cauchy–Schwarz step holds exactly when \(X\) and \(Y\) are linearly dependent over \(\mathbb C\). Writing \(Y=cX\) gives \(g_\Pi(V,V)=\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\), \(g_\Pi(W,W)=|c|^2\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\), \(g_\Pi(V,W)=\mathrm{Re}(\bar c)\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\) and \(\Omega_\Pi(V,W)=-2\,\mathrm{Im}(\bar c)\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\); both sides of (6.1) equal \(2|\mathrm{Im}\,c|\,\|X\|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}\). Setting \(c=i\) gives the stated pair. ∎

In coordinate form, saturation of the determinant inequality is known to occur exactly when the parametric tensor acquires a null vector, and bands saturating it everywhere are called Kähler, ideal or vortexable. Corollary 6.2 is that criterion in block language: \(Y=cX\) is the statement that the Gram matrix of \(V,W\) is singular, and the extremal partner can be written down explicitly, in every rank, as the tangent vector whose block is \(iX\). Pulled back along a two-parameter family, (6.1) is \(4\det g_{ij}\ge\Omega_{12}^2\).

6.1 The geometric angle safeguard

Expressing the metric inner product as \(g_\Pi(V,W)=\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)g_\Pi(W,W)}\,\cos\theta_G\), where \(\theta_G\) is the geometric angle between the two tangent deformations, turns (6.1) into

\[|\Omega_\Pi(V,W)|\ \le\ 2\sqrt{g_\Pi(V,V)\,g_\Pi(W,W)}\;|\sin\theta_G| . \tag{6.2}\]

This \(\sin\theta_G\) dependence is a directional safeguard: the curvature a pair of deformations can generate is limited by how far apart they point in the intrinsic metric. The control it gives is relative, not absolute. Near a degeneracy both sides of (6.2) diverge at the same rate — a two-level system saturates the bound at every gap, so the ratio stays one as the gap closes — and the inequality is not a regularisation. The suppression at \(\sin\theta_G=0\) is a property of antisymmetry, since any 2-form vanishes on proportional arguments.

6.2 Control hierarchy

Both geometric quantities are driven by the same interband transition amplitudes \(\langle m|\delta\Sigma|n\rangle\) across the spectral cut (Theorem 3.6). The quantum metric quantifies the total capacity of the transport channels; the Berry curvature tracks the phase interference among them; and the metric is the quantitative envelope for every admissible curvature. Metric rigidity therefore enforces curvature rigidity: if \(g_\Pi(V,V)=0\) then \(V=0\) and \(\Omega_\Pi(V,\cdot)=0\), and if a perturbation has no cross-gap coupling, \(\langle m|\delta\Sigma|n\rangle=0\) for every \(m\in\mathcal I,\ n\in\mathcal I^c\), then it moves nothing and carries no curvature. The ordering is one of control, not of generation: cross-gap transitions generate both \(g\) and \(\Omega\), and \(g\) bounds \(\Omega\).
